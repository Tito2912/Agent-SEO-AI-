Link building has a bad reputation, and it earned it. The version that works is slow,
unglamorous, and rests on one simple question: **why would anyone cite your site?**

If you don't have an answer, no outreach technique will make up for it.

## First: have a reason to be cited

People link to resources, not to companies. What gets cited naturally:

- a piece of **data** no one else publishes — a study of your customers, real prices in your
  market, a benchmark;
- a **free tool**, even a modest one: a calculator, a template, a downloadable
  checklist;
- a **go-to explanation** of a technical topic in your field, the one people link to rather
  than explaining it all over again;
- a **case study with real numbers**, failures included. That's what gets shared the most,
  because no one else publishes it.

Without one of these four, you're not doing link building: you're asking for a favor.

## Where to look, from most to least effective

**1. Unlinked mentions.** Search for your brand name: some sites already mention you without
linking to you. This has the highest success rate in all of link building, because the person
knows you and the ask is tiny.

**2. Your competitors' links.** A site that cited a competitor on a topic has shown that it
covers that topic. If your resource is better or more recent, the pitch is easy to make.

**3. Resource pages.** “Best tools for X,” “guides to Y.” They exist to list things, and their
authors actively try to keep them up to date.

**4. Broken links that used to point to content like yours.** You do them a favor before asking
for anything — it's the best effort-to-results ratio after unlinked mentions.

**5. Discussions where your topic comes up.** Forums, communities. Handle with care: a link
dropped without taking part in the conversation is spam, and it gets treated as such.

## The message

Three lines, not thirty. What works:

- **A specific, verifiable reason.** “Your article cites a 2019 study; we published one on the
  same data in 2026.”
- **The exact link**, and where it would fit on their page.
- **Nothing offered in return.** Systematic link swaps get spotted, and they hurt both
  sites.

What doesn't work, and lands in everyone's inbox every day: “I read your excellent article”
without naming the article, a paragraph about your company, a link exchange request.

!!! warning "The volume math doesn't add up"
    Sending 300 generic messages for 3 replies costs more than 20 targeted messages for
    4 replies — in time, in reputation, and in the risk of landing on an email provider's
    blocklist. Volume isn't a strategy; it's an admission.

## Afterward: monitor

This is the step almost no one takes, and it pays off more than outreach.

A link you've earned isn't yours for good. The page gets redesigned, the article is
unpublished, the link switches to `nofollow`, or it disappears without explanation. A link
profile naturally loses a few percent a year.

Revisit the pages that link to you periodically. A lost link can often be recovered with a
polite message — far more easily than earning a new one.

## What to check before you start

Before you spend a cent or an hour on link building, make sure the pages you want to push
are:

- **indexable** — a link to a `noindex` page is money down the drain;
- **properly titled**, because the traffic that arrives will read the `title` elsewhere too;
- **connected to the rest of the site**, so the authority you earn flows through it.

A technical audit costs a day. A link building campaign pointing at broken pages costs far
more.
